# Portfolio presentation preferences

- Make the central problem statement or brief clearly bold on project pages. Do not invent a problem for art collections or internship selections.
- Stacked Streets is a detailed recent project: retain its masterplan analysis, individual intentions and target-user build-up in concise paragraphs, rather than converting it wholesale to bullets. Credit the masterplan as collective studio work. Omit precedent studies as requested.

- Maximum image size: use the Wandering Archive selected cave site image as the reference, at half the content width on desktop. Keep individual images at or below this size, centred where standalone; reduce diagrams rather than making them full-width. On narrow screens use the available width. Apply this to future portfolio edits.

- Align the top and bottom image boundaries within each side-by-side row. Put captions below the shared image baseline.
- For one large image beside a stack, align the large image's top and bottom with the combined stack's outer image edges.
- Preserve drawing geometry and labels; contain technical drawings rather than cropping them. Render photos can use a considered crop when necessary.
- Keep the external editor-style frame and selection handles flush with visible image edges. Remove embedded export borders without leaving inset gaps.
- Apply the flush-frame rule to every photo, render, diagram and drawing, including future additions. If an image is contained within a larger layout cell, put its border and handles on the actual visible image rectangle, not the cell. Do not add white padding between artwork and frame or crop drawing labels to fill a frame.
- Keep all eight selection squares fully visible: image links, frames and figure wrappers must not clip their overflow.
- Text-heavy diagrams may exceed the usual half-width image limit for readability. The Wandering Archive personal timeline and data-flow diagram use 75% width on desktop, centred.
- Preserve these alignments on future edits; stack images cleanly on narrow screens.
