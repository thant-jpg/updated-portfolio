Thant Thiri Wai
Bukit Dermaga explores the potential of the Kallang River as 
a connective urban landscape that brings ecological systems, 
infrastructure, and housing into closer spatial and functional alignment. 
Rather than treating the river as a fixed edge or singular public space, 
this project positions it as an active framework through which urban 
life, environmental processes, and diverse forms of habitation can 
coexist in harmony. The masterplan is structured around a 40% water 
and 60% landscape framework, establishing blue–green infrastructure 
as the primary organiser of the precinct. Water and landscape are 
understood not only as environmental assets but as spatial systems 
capable of shaping circulation, public space, and architectural form. 
Through this approach, the river becomes a capillary network that 
distributes ecological continuity and programmatic intensity across the 
site. Aligned with the objectives of the URA Identity Corridor, the project 
interprets identity as something produced through use, adaptation, and 
spatial diversity. Ten adaptive infill parcels are positioned along the 
river corridor, each responding to specific urban and environmental 
conditions—including maritime infrastructure, transport remnants, 
mangrove ecologies, cliff edges, and water thresholds. Selective 
demolition, adaptive reuse, and landscape terraforming allow new 
interventions to engage productively with existing structures and 
terrains. Rather than applying a uniform housing model, the precinct 
brings together a range of housing typologies and intensities. These 
include over-water modular collectives, terraced housing integrated 
with ecological systems, market-infrastructure hybrids, maritime 
live-work environments, and vertically layered residential forms. 
Together, these typologies support a broad demographic spectrum

while maintaining strong relationships to landscape and water. 
Across the precinct, density is approached as distributed and layered, 
extending across ground, building, and river rather than concentrating 
on traditional and vernacular building practices. Public spaces are 
embedded within the housing fabric, and ecological systems are 
integrated as active components of daily life. Human and non-human 
actors are considered together, allowing the precinct to function as a 
socio-ecological system rather than a collection of isolated buildings.  
Collectively, the ten projects form a flexible and regenerative urban 
framework that enhances habitat continuity, supports diverse modes 
of living, and integrates public life within a high-density environment. 
The river is repositioned as connective civic infrastructure—linking 
landscape, housing, and identity into a cohesive urban system.
