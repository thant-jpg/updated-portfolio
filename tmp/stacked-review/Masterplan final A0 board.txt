BUKIT DERMAGA
masterplan
Bukit Dermaga explores the potential of the Kallang 
River as a connective urban landscape that brings 
ecological systems, infrastructure, and housing 
into closer spatial and functional alignment. Rather 
than treating the river as a fixed edge or singular 
public space, this project positions it as an active 
framework through which urban life, environmental 
processes, and diverse forms of habitation can 
coexist in harmony. The masterplan is structured 
around a 40% water and 60% landscape framework, 
establishing blue–green infrastructure as the primary 
organiser of the precinct. Water and landscape are 
understood not only as environmental assets but 
as spatial systems capable of shaping circulation, 
public space, and architectural form. Through this 
approach, the river becomes a capillary network that 
distributes ecological continuity and programmatic 
intensity across the site. Aligned with the objectives 
of the URA Identity Corridor, the project interprets 
identity as something produced through use, 
adaptation, and spatial diversity. Ten adaptive infill 
parcels are positioned along the river corridor, each 
responding to specific urban and environmental 
conditions—including maritime infrastructure, 
transport remnants, mangrove ecologies, cliff edges, 
and water thresholds. Selective demolition, adaptive 
reuse, and landscape terraforming allow new 
interventions to engage productively with existing 
structures and terrains. Rather than applying a 
uniform housing model, the precinct brings together 
a range of housing typologies and intensities. 
These include over-water modular collectives, 
terraced housing integrated with ecological systems, 
market-infrastructure hybrids, maritime live-work 
environments, and vertically layered residential 
forms. Together, these typologies support a broad 
demographic spectrum while maintaining strong 
relationships to landscape and water.
